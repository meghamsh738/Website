01 / ResearchBrain–immune interactions

How does the brain
find its way back?

Inflammation. Ageing. Recovery.

My research explores systemic inflammation, vulnerability to delirium in ageing, and the dynamics of recovery.

I study how TNF-α-driven inflammation relates to these processes, and how IL-17A may modulate recovery. This work connects neuroimmunology with behaviour and brain network function.

Research focus in the 2026 TCD programme
NeuroimmunologySystemic inflammationAgeingBrain networks

Earlier research · Hiyaa Ghosh lab, NCBS

Understanding microglial states.

I studied how microglia maintain homeostasis and respond to brain injury, combining single-cell analysis with experiments that follow cells over time.

01

Single-cell RNA sequencing

I established an R analysis pipeline and analysed both lab-generated and public datasets to compare microglial states across injury and genetic perturbations.

02

BrdU pulse–chase and lineage tracing

I used BrdU pulse–chase to follow dividing cells through the injury response, alongside tamoxifen-induced genetic lineage tracing in mouse models.

03

Imaging and flow cytometry

I characterised cellular changes using immunofluorescence, Leica SP8 confocal microscopy, marker-intensity analysis and flow cytometry.

The analysis work also led me to write ImageJ and Jython macros for image analysis and data extraction.

03 / A little backgroundScience, with a builder’s mindset

Curiosity is
the common thread.

I’m a neuroimmunology researcher at Trinity College Dublin, with earlier research experience at the National Centre for Biological Sciences in Bengaluru.

At NCBS, I studied microglial states and homeostasis in Hiyaa Ghosh’s lab. That work brought together experiments, single-cell analysis and image-analysis code—a combination that continues to shape how I approach research and build software.
