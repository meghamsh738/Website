About Me

The mind behind the microscope

I'm a PhD student in Colm Cunningham's Lab at Trinity College Dublin, where I investigate the mechanisms underlying delirium — a debilitating acute neurological syndrome associated with systemic illness — through the lens of neuroimmunology and glial biology.

Before joining TCD, I was a Junior Research Fellow in Hiyaa Ghosh's Lab at NCBS Bangalore, where I worked on microglial dynamics and fate in injury response using single-cell transcriptomics and advanced imaging techniques.

Beyond the bench, I'm passionate about coding, automation, and bringing AI-driven approaches to biological research. I also have a deep interest in mechanical engineering and enjoy blending analytical thinking with creative problem-solving.

Research

Investigating the brain's immune guardians

Current Position

PhD Researcher — Delirium & Neuroinflammation

Colm Cunningham Lab · Trinity College Dublin

Ongoing

Investigating the neuroimmunological underpinnings of delirium in the context of systemic inflammation and surgery. My work focuses on how peripheral immune signals propagate to the brain, perturb glial homeostasis, and drive acute cognitive dysfunction in vulnerable populations.

⚡ Glial cell activation in systemic illness
⚡ Molecular mechanisms of acute CNS dysfunction
⚡ Neuroimmune communication pathways
Previous Position

Junior Research Fellow — Microglial Dynamics

Hiyaa Ghosh Lab · NCBS Bangalore

Completed

Explored microglial fate and phenotypic plasticity following CNS injury using single-cell RNA sequencing and advanced imaging modalities. Contributed to understanding how microglia transition between homeostatic and reactive states and the transcriptional programs governing these transitions.

⚡ Single-cell transcriptomics of microglia
⚡ Microglial fate mapping post-injury
⚡ Flow cytometry & immunofluorescence imaging
